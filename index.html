Preparing public institutions for transformative AI

An independent nonprofit working to ensure governments can use advanced AI safely and accountably.

Our work Support us

Mission

Public institutions run benefit schemes for the most vulnerable, keep the legal environment stable, enforce the law and hold elections. AI could make them far more capable. It could also leave them unable to do these jobs, overwhelmed by actors who use AI faster than they can respond, or unable to be held to account, as AI expands executive power beyond the reach of public checks.

Our mission is to prepare public institutions for transformative AI, so they can act effectively and still answer to the public.

Work

Preparing public institutions for transformative AI depends on getting many legal, technical and organisational choices right: which tasks AI agents take on, how services and processes are redesigned around them, what they may do in the state’s name, how they are tested, and who can stop them. Most of these choices are made in and around government digital teams. Our work supports these teams in three ways: research that gives them the tools and concepts they need, hands-on support as they adopt and regulate AI, and a network that connects them with each other and with AI researchers.

Where it started

The Agentic State
Vision Paper

We launched the Vision Paper at the Tallinn Digital Summit in October 2025, with the support of the World Bank and the Global GovTech Centre Berlin. More than 20 digital government leaders from 15 countries wrote it with us.

It was the first framework for how governments can adopt agentic AI while keeping public power accountable. It works through twelve layers of government, from service design, workflows and rule-making to agent governance, cybersecurity and procurement, and sets out the technical, organisational and leadership changes each one needs. Governments in Ukraine, Italy, Japan and elsewhere have since used it to plan their own work.
