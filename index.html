01 — Why

What is at stake

Public institutions run benefit schemes for the most vulnerable, keep the legal environment stable, enforce the law and hold elections. AI could make them far more capable. It could also leave them unable to do these jobs, or unable to be held to account for how they do them.

Decay

Actors using AI faster than institutions can respond overwhelm and hollow them out. Early signs are visible already: AI-generated applications, complaints and filings that exploit procedural, legal and technical weak points at a volume public offices cannot absorb.

Capture

AI deployed inside government expands executive power and erodes accountability. When no one outside a single leader, agency or vendor can audit or stop the systems acting in the state’s name, public power stops answering to the public.

We work on institutions that hold up against both. Against decay, governments need to use AI well enough to protect people and services from AI-empowered actors. Against capture, the AI they deploy has to stay contestable, with checks that no single leader, agency or vendor can switch off.

Most of the choices that decide this are made by the teams building government digital systems: which tasks agents take on, what they may do in the state’s name, how they are tested, and who can stop them. These teams get little attention from the AI safety field. We work with them directly.

02 — What we do

Three lines of work

We do research, work hands-on with governments, and build the field that connects public leaders with people working to make AI go well.

Research

The tools and concepts public institutions need to use AI without losing control of it, and how public missions must change as capabilities grow.

  • The Agentic State Vision Paper
  • The AGI State, with Google DeepMind researchers
  • Agent Governance Card
  • RADAR, with the World Bank

Government engagements

Hands-on work with the teams deciding which tasks agents take on, what they may do and who can stop them, so these choices are made well where they are made.

  • Ukraine: national roadmap
  • Finland: process readiness study
  • United States: agent governance with state teams
  • South Africa: readiness assessment, with the World Bank

Field building

Putting preparedness for advanced AI on the agenda of ministers and government CIOs, and connecting them with frontier researchers.

  • The Agentic State Observatory
  • A network of ministers, CIOs and CTOs on five continents
  • Tallinn Digital Summit and other high-level forums

03 — How we work

Independent, and in the room

Government work is central to how we create change, but we set our own agenda.

We take on work that serves our mission. Governments, multilaterals and research partners fund specific pieces of work; philanthropic support funds the shared agenda.

We publish what we learn. Every engagement should produce tools, evidence or methods that other governments can use, including those that cannot pay for them.

We disclose who funds what. Each piece of work names the organisations that supported it.

Where it started

The Agentic State
Vision Paper

Launched at the Tallinn Digital Summit 2025 and written with more than 20 digital government leaders, the Vision Paper is the first framework for how governments can adopt agentic AI while keeping public power accountable. It maps twelve layers of government, from service design and procurement to agent governance and cybersecurity.
